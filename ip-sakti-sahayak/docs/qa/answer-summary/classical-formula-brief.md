## Short answer

In India, an unchanged traditional formula generally cannot be patented: section 3(p) excludes traditional knowledge and merely repeating the known properties of traditional ingredients. Absence from classical books alone does not overcome this exclusion. [patents_3p]

A genuinely new formulation, improvement or manufacturing process can be assessed separately. It must be new, involve an inventive step and be industrially usable; calling it new does not establish those tests or overcome patent exclusions. [patents_invention]

Simply mixing ingredients whose effects only add together is also excluded, as is a process for making that mere mixture. Evidence of an effect beyond that may help, but synergy alone does not guarantee a patent. [patents_3e]

The regulatory term 'patent or proprietary medicine' is not a patent grant. This ASU category requires all ingredients to appear in First-Schedule book formulae, the formulation itself to be absent from those formulae, and a non-parenteral route. These conditions are separate from invention patentability. [dc_3h]

## Detailed explanation

**Classical or proprietary medicine**

Established by source: Section 3(a) covers ASU medicines intended for internal or external use in the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease or disorder in humans or animals, and manufactured exclusively according to formulae in the authoritative books listed in the First Schedule. (India; s.3(a)) [dc_3a] An ASU patent or proprietary medicine must contain only ingredients mentioned in formulae in First-Schedule authoritative books; the formulation itself must not be included there, and parenteral medicines are excluded. (India; s.3(h)(i)) [dc_3h]

Inference/application: The classical ASU route first requires medicinal intended use for diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease or disorder. A formula match alone does not establish that use. Classical status remains unresolved: compare the complete formula and manufacture with a First-Schedule formula. Developing a combination yourself or studying classical texts does not establish either a match or its absence. [dc_3a] First establish that the product is an ASU medicine on its actual intended use; an ingredient-book match alone does not classify a nonmedicinal product as a medicine. The proprietary category is then conditional on every ingredient meeting the First-Schedule book test, the formulation itself being absent from those formulae, and a non-parenteral route. Calling the product new or proprietary does not verify those facts or establish patentability. [dc_3h]

Not established by retrieved sources: The actual intended use, complete formula, manufacture and comparison with the relevant authoritative books require verification; formula matching alone cannot establish this category. Ingredient identities, their book references and the administration route are needed to confirm eligibility; describing plants as Ayurvedic is insufficient.

**Patent eligibility for the formulation and process**

Established by source: A patentable invention must be a new product or process involving an inventive step and capable of industrial application; the Act separately defines inventive step and new invention. (India; ss.2(1)(j), 2(1)(ja), 2(1)(l)) [patents_invention] Section 48 provides the specified exclusive rights for patented products and processes in India; ss.73–74 identify the Controller of Patents and Patent Office. (India; ss.48, 73-74) [patents_rights]

Inference/application: Assess the specific formulation or manufacturing-process claims for novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability. The stated development history or a new product label does not establish those tests. [patents_invention] A patent may protect qualifying product/process claims, but does not itself establish regulatory permission to sell or freedom to operate against other patents. [patents_rights]

Not established by retrieved sources: No prior-art search, claim analysis or experimental evidence has established that this particular invention satisfies those tests. The scope of proposed claims, third-party rights and regulatory clearance require separate assessments.

**Patent exclusions**

Established by source: Traditional knowledge, and aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known components, are excluded from inventions by s.3(p). (India; s.3(p)) [patents_3p] Section 3(e) excludes a mere admixture giving only an aggregation of component properties and a process for producing that substance. (India; s.3(e)) [patents_3e] Section 3(d) excludes a new form of a known substance without enhancement of known efficacy and other specified discoveries involving known substances or processes. (India; s.3(d)) [patents_3d] Section 3(i) excludes processes for specified medicinal and other treatment of humans and similar treatment of animals. (India; s.3(i)) [patents_3i] Section 3(j) excludes plants and animals in whole or part, including seeds, varieties and species, and essentially biological production or propagation processes, subject to its micro-organism exception. (India; s.3(j)) [patents_3j]

Inference/application: Compare the proposed claims with traditional knowledge and the known properties of traditionally known components; a new product description does not establish that this exclusion is overcome. [patents_3p] A mixture that only adds the known effects of its herbs may fail this exclusion; evidence of an interaction beyond that aggregation is relevant to the assessment. [patents_3e] Review this exclusion if the proposed claim concerns a new form, use or process involving a known substance; a new label or dosage is not enough to decide patentability. [patents_3d] A claim to a treatment or administration regimen can engage this exclusion; a product or manufacturing-process claim must be assessed on its own terms. [patents_3i] A plant, plant part or variety is not made patentable simply by being used in a new medicine; formulation/process eligibility requires a separate assessment. [patents_3j]

Not established by retrieved sources: Whether the proposed claims merely reproduce traditional knowledge requires a claim-specific prior-art comparison. The sources do not establish that this formulation has such an effect or that evidence of synergy alone would guarantee a patent. The identity of the known substance, the precise claims and the relevant efficacy evidence are not supplied. No proposed claim wording is available to determine whether a dosage feature is claimed as a product feature or a treatment method. The exact subject matter of any plant-related claims remains unspecified.
