## Short answer

India: Confidential process know-how may be protected through secrecy and confidentiality agreements, but publicly known or readily discoverable information needs a different assessment. Secrecy cannot replace mandatory patent or ingredient disclosures. Check what can actually remain confidential and whether the agreements are enforceable. [trade_secret]

Section 11A provides for publication of patent applications subject to its exceptions; the Act's anticipation provisions contain specific, limited exceptions for certain disclosures. Public disclosure can affect novelty. Relying on secrecy after publication, or assuming a broad grace period for every disclosure, would be unsafe. [patents_disclosure]

The cultivated medicinal-plant exemption in section 7 requires the prescribed BMC certificate of origin. Retain a certificate for the actual cultivated material rather than relying only on a supplier's assertion. [bda_s7_exemption]

In India, a patent does not itself give permission to sell the medicine or establish freedom to use other people's patented inventions. It protects the qualifying product or process claims that are granted; regulatory clearance and other patent rights need separate checks. [patents_rights]

**Step-by-step strategy**

1. Inference/application — Preserve confidentiality and decide what to disclose: Limit access to non-public formulae and process parameters, use appropriate confidentiality agreements and document who receives the information. [trade_secret] Give the patent drafter the reproducible method, relevant best-method details, biological-material sources and geographical origins. [patents_specification]

2. Inference/application — Confirm the product category and licensing path: Check the actual medicinal intended use and compare the complete formula and manufacture with the First-Schedule books before choosing a licensing category. [dc_3a] Prepare an ingredient-by-ingredient list of authoritative-book references and record the administration route for regulatory review. [dc_3h]

3. Inference/application — Resolve material sourcing and access obligations: Record incorporation, ownership/control and relevant residency facts, then assess s.3 before the proposed access. [bda_s3] Create a material-by-material sourcing record: identity, source/location, supplier, wild or cultivated status, access date and intended use. [bda_scope]

4. Inference/application — Assess the proposed patent claims: Document the claimed technical contribution and commission a prior-art and patentability assessment before selecting claims. [patents_invention] Assess patentability and freedom to operate separately, and coordinate any patent application with the Patent Office route. [patents_rights]

5. Inference/application — Plan biodiversity IP and benefit-sharing steps: Identify the applicant category and plan the corresponding NBA approval/registration before grant and, where s.6(1B) applies, approval at commercialisation. [bda_s6] Check the applicable NBA form and ensure the IP applicant identity and resource-origin records support the filing. [bda_ipr_forms]

6. Inference/application — Select the applicable brand and other IP protection: Choose a distinctive brand and conduct clearance before applying for the relevant goods/services. [tm_act] Keep dated artwork/text files and confirm ownership or assignments for commissioned creative material. [copyright_act]
