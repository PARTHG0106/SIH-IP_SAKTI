# IP-SAKTI Sahayak — revised research brief (5)

Question: I am an Ayurvedic manufacturer in India planning to launch a new proprietary Ayurvedic medicine for joint pain. The formulation contains 5 medicinal plants, and the exact combination and manufacturing process were developed by us after studying several classical Ayurvedic texts. Two of the ingredients are sourced from wild plants collected in India, while the other three are cultivated by our suppliers.

The product will be manufactured in India and sold under a new brand name. We also want to eventually export it to the United States and European Union.

Please analyse the situation comprehensively and explain:

Whether the product could qualify as a classical Ayurvedic medicine, a patent/proprietary medicine, or another regulatory category.
Which parts of the formulation or manufacturing process could potentially receive patent protection and which parts could be excluded because of traditional knowledge, known substances, admixtures, plants, treatment methods, or other relevant exclusions.
Whether studying classical Ayurvedic texts creates any traditional-knowledge or prior-art concern.
What IP protection could potentially be used for the brand, packaging, formulation, manufacturing know-how, technical documents and artwork.
For the two wild-sourced Indian plants and three cultivated plants, explain how the Biological Diversity Act could apply differently and what facts about sourcing, cultivation and suppliers need to be established.
Explain the possible roles of the National Biodiversity Authority, State Biodiversity Board, Biodiversity Management Committee and AYUSH/State licensing authority.
Explain what records and evidence I should maintain for the biological materials, including source, geographical origin, quantities, cultivation status and supplier information.
Explain what could change if the formulation is later patented and commercialised.
Identify the major facts that you still need from me before any definitive conclusion can be made.

Use current Indian primary/government sources wherever available. For every major conclusion, cite the exact section, rule or notification. Clearly separate established law, inference/application to my facts, and issues that remain unverified. Do not assume that all medicinal plants are treated identically under the biodiversity framework.
